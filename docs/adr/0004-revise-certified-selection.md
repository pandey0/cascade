# ADR 0004: Revise certified selection (strict-start fixed sequence has a power flaw)
Status: accepted (2026-11-01).
**Context.** ADR 0003 chose fixed-sequence testing walking from the strictest threshold. Experiment 1a showed it deploys almost never: the strictest non-empty thresholds accept few items, so even zero errors give a loose upper bound (0 errors in 37 items: 6.0% at delta 0.1) and the walk stops before reaching thresholds with enough data. My earlier statement that certification "needs thousands of audited examples" was partly caused by this.
**Decision.** Default certified gate = **Bonferroni over a fixed 32-point grid** (`select_bonferroni`): valid by the union bound, no ordering assumption, no early stop. Offer `select_split_sequence` (safe start chosen on a dev split, then fixed-sequence on the rest) and keep `select_certified` (strict start) only as a labelled baseline. All grids are fixed before seeing data.
**Consequences.** Validity unchanged (P(violation) <= delta under i.i.d. calibration data). Observed Bonferroni violation rates are far below delta, so a more powerful valid method (Holm step-down; data-independent starting point; cross-fitting) is a clear next step. Cost of validity measured on real tasks: about 4-13 coverage points at 1,000-2,000 audit labels (see `docs/notes/exp1a-findings.md`).

## Outcome of the follow-up run (5 splits, 8 gates)
Holm added at most about 1 coverage point over Bonferroni on these tasks; both held observed violations at or below 0.5%. An uncorrected Wilson margin stayed near the nominal level in these i.i.d. settings with higher coverage, so it is a reasonable choice when no formal guarantee is required. Decision: **recommend Holm as the certified default** (`select_holm`), keep Bonferroni as the simpler alternative, document Wilson as a heuristic baseline. See `docs/notes/exp1a-findings.md`.
